What goes into one cell

Slope and roughness come from the elevation grid. The elevation is perturbed with correlated noise to get a slope spread. A terrain classifier gives class probabilities, so hazard is a probability, not a yes or no. Steep or very likely hazardous cells are hard blocked, and that rule is separate from the soft cost weights.

Deliverable 2

Planned path

सुरक्षित मार्ग

The planner prices each step with a bad-case average of its cost (CVaR). One dial moves the rover from bold to cautious. A safety margin around blocked ground grows with distance driven, because position estimates drift. The amber band is a corridor of near-equal routes, so a drifting rover still has room.

Deliverable 3

Science targets

खोज के लक्ष्य

Each candidate is scored on hydration signal, ice prior and geological interest, minus the risk of getting there. We resample the noisy inputs and the weights 1000 times, so every target gets a range of ranks, not a single number. Overlapping intervals are flagged as ties.

Targets, hydration and ice values are simulated, as are the spectral layers. They show the method, not real Mars evidence. Hover a target to read its evidence.

What we bring to the problem

Key features

हमारी खूबियाँ

+

Localization uncertainty

A rover without GPS drifts the further it drives. The safety margin around blocked ground grows with distance travelled, following Sanchez-Ibanez et al. (IROS 2022), so longer routes get wider berths.

Shown on this page
+

Risk you can dial

Steps are priced by the bad-case average of their cost, and hazards are probabilities that combine slope, roughness and terrain class. One dial moves the rover from bold to cautious. Hard safety rules stay apart from soft weights.

Shown on this page
+

Doubt in every layer

The elevation grid is perturbed with correlated noise to see where slopes cannot be trusted, including missing patches. Tree votes from a Random Forest give model uncertainty. A small SegFormer on rover images is an optional side track.

In the Python build
+

Corridors, not lines

Two shortest-path runs, from the start and from the goal, give every route within a few percent of optimal. That band survives drift and surprises between uplinks.

Shown on this page
+

Ranks you can question

1000 resamples give each target a rank interval and a chance of making the top three, with ties flagged. Every result carries an evidence string, a safety check confirms the path avoids hard hazards, and honest baselines sit beside our model.

Shown on this page
+

Research we stand on

Brad and Balog 2025 (terrain A*, our weak baseline). Sanchez-Ibanez et al. 2022 (drift margin). Endo et al. 2023 (class probabilities with CVaR). AI4Mars, JPL SPOC, Perseverance AutoNav. HiRISE, CRISM and SWIM data.

Why we are doing this

Our motivation

भारत का अगला कदम, मंगल की सतह पर

India reached Mars orbit on its first attempt with Mangalyaan in 2014, and put Vikram and the Pragyan rover on the Moon with Chandrayaan-3 in 2023. A rover on the surface of Mars is the natural next question, and it is a harder one. Signals take minutes each way, so the rover has to make its own decisions.

We are students working on one small piece of that question: how a rover can plan safely, and choose where to look, while being honest about what it cannot see. We hope the ideas here, the risk dial, the drift margin and the rank intervals, are useful to people building the real thing.

This is a hackathon project. It is not affiliated with ISRO, and nothing here is a claim about real rover safety.

Limits we state openly

One elevation tile, one rover, a static map. No wheel slip or power model.

Spectral, ice and noise layers are simulated. Slope limits and cost weights are assumptions, unverified against real rover data. The CVaR cost is computed edge by edge from a Gaussian assumption, so it is cautious but not a formal safety guarantee.

The maps on this page run a small in-browser version of the method on simulated terrain. The Python pipeline is the real build.
